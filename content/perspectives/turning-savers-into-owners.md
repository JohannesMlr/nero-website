title: Turning Savers into Owners: The Case for a European Investment Culture
teaser: Europeans save more than Americans and own far less. Closing that gap could be the single biggest lever for European wealth and growth.
lede: Trillions of euros sit in low-yield accounts across the continent while European companies raise growth capital abroad. The fix is cultural as much as regulatory.
pull: How can we turn Europeans into owners and leverage private capital to fuel growth and public goods within a unified market?

The numbers are almost embarrassing in their simplicity: European households hold a smaller share of their savings in capital markets than almost any comparable region, while European startups routinely cross the Atlantic to raise the capital that will fund their growth. The savings exist. The plumbing to connect them to opportunity does not.

Part of the fix is regulatory — a genuine single market for capital, not twenty-seven overlapping ones. Part of it is product: retirement and investment vehicles designed for ordinary savers, not just institutions. And part of it is cultural — treating ownership of European growth as a civic good, not a gamble reserved for professionals.

Countries that have made this shift — turning savers into shareholders through simple, well-designed vehicles — have seen both household wealth and domestic investment rise together. There's no reason that has to remain a national story rather than a European one.
