title: Deterrence Is a Design Problem
teaser: Europe doesn't lack the industrial base to defend itself. It lacks the shared design discipline to turn that base into deterrence.
lede: Rebuilding military capability is only half the challenge. The harder half is designing systems — procurement, industry, doctrine — that can act as one.
pull: How can we quickly rebuild the military force and resilience to prevent attacks and defend Europe as an independent power?
---

Europe's defense industry is larger and more capable than its output suggests. Dozens of programmes, calibres and standards coexist across the continent, each defensible on its own terms and collectively a drag on the speed and scale that deterrence requires.

The postwar generation built institutions on the assumption that peace was the default state to protect. The next generation has to build institutions on the assumption that resilience must be actively designed — in supply chains, in reserves, in the civilian systems an adversary would target first.

This is uncomfortable territory for a continent that spent decades treating defense as an American given. But the states moving fastest now are the ones treating resilience as an engineering problem to be solved this decade, not a political debate to be managed indefinitely.
