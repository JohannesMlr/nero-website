For a decade, European energy policy was written in the language of dependency: which pipeline, which supplier, which crisis. That framing is starting to give way to a more confident one, built around a simple bet — that a continent with world-class engineering, capital markets and manufacturing can build its way to abundance rather than merely manage scarcity.

Getting there means treating permitting speed as seriously as subsidy design. Projects that take a decade to approve lose to capital that can deploy in two years elsewhere. It also means an honest conversation about the full mix — renewables, grid, storage and nuclear — rather than picking technologies for political comfort.

Done right, this is not just a climate story. Cheap, sovereign, abundant energy is the foundation under every other ambition on this list — from reshoring industry to powering the compute that AI leadership requires.
