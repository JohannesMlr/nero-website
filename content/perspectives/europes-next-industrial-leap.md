teaser: Europe has the talent and industrial depth to lead the next wave of technology — but only if it stops treating deep tech as a national project.

---

Walk into any leading AI lab, quantum computing startup or advanced materials facility in Europe, and you'll find no shortage of ambition. What's missing is scale. A researcher in Munich and a manufacturer in Lyon operate in different funding systems, different regulatory regimes and, often, different languages of ambition. The result is a continent that invents at a world-class level and commercialises at a regional one.

The fix isn't more funding alone — Europe already spends heavily on research. It's coordination: shared testbeds for frontier technologies, procurement rules that reward European scale-ups instead of the cheapest bidder, and a single market for talent that lets the best people move to wherever the hardest problems are being solved.

None of this requires new bureaucracy so much as it requires nerve — a willingness to back European champions before they're proven, the way competitors already do. The countries and companies that make that bet first will set the terms for everyone else.
