# New European Narrative and Consciousness — brief

## Current state

Europe's narrative deficit is not a shortage of stories. It is a shortage of stated destinations.
Political communication across the continent has settled into a defensive register — securing pensions,
securing peace, securing prosperity — and that is a promise to preserve, not an invitation to build.
A continent that offers only preservation cannot generate aspiration, however well it tells its story.
This field's guiding question therefore has a dependency: an aspirational story of Europe presupposes a
collective answer to who we are together and what we want to become, and that answer is currently
missing rather than badly told.

The competitive picture makes the gap visible. China pursues expansive five-year plans with
considerable confidence; large parts of the United States rally behind making America great again.
Both are, whatever else they are, functioning collective narratives with a direction attached. Europe
by contrast often appears unsure whether it has joint goals at all — which means the continent is not
losing a narrative contest so much as declining to enter one.

The more uncomfortable diagnosis is that this is not merely a communications failure by a generation of
politicians. Avoiding shared goals is a rational response to a pluralistic, individualistic society that
no longer has a settled answer to the identity question — and where elected leadership vacates that
question, it does not go unanswered. It gets answered by more radical actors, and the resulting conflicts
amplify inside echo chambers instead of being worked through in public. Since every social conflict is at
bottom a conflict of goals, a polity cannot actually be goal-free; it can only be one where goals are set
somewhere other than in its democratic institutions.

What the field can build on: the mechanism connecting narrative to resilience is not speculative.
El-Mafaalani's argument in *Misstrauensgemeinschaften* is that societies with a positive outlook on the
future can absorb considerable hardship and endure long periods of strain — and that spirals of mutual
distrust and radicalisation begin only once confidence in a better tomorrow erodes. That reframes the
aspirational story from decoration into infrastructure: it is what buys a society the tolerance for the
reforms every other action field is asking for. Kennedy's moonshot remains the standing case study for a
single ambitious goal mobilising a nation and unlocking resources and invention far beyond what was
plausible at the outset — and the Adenauer / Brandt / Kohl pattern (Western integration, rapprochement
with the East, reunification and European integration) shows the same at national scale: a focused
overriding goal produces a brand, an agenda and a political culture capable of outlasting a single
legislative period. Simplicity is doing the work in all of these — few people retain more than two or
three attributes of any identity, national ones included.

Open front: the identity layer itself is deferred in everything we have filed so far. Our own writing
explicitly parks "who are we, together?" for a companion essay that does not yet exist, and until it
does, this field has a well-argued account of *why* the story is missing and no draft of the story.

## Open questions / what to watch next

- The announced companion essay on collective identity, local and European, is the single most important unwritten piece for this field. Everything here currently argues around the gap rather than filling it.
- Does an aspirational European story follow direction or create it? If narrative follows goals, this field is downstream of 03 and should say so; if it leads, it needs its own source of ambition.
- Who supplies Europe's most-heard story of itself today, and on what emotional register? We should be able to name the competition precisely rather than gesture at "populists".
- Is there a European equivalent of the moonshot available — a single, legible, ambitious goal that would do narrative work across member states — or does European identity only ever assemble from many smaller ones?
- The pattern says two or three attributes is the ceiling for what people retain. What are Europe's two or three, stated positively, without reaching for "peace project" or "values"?
- Counter-signal to keep honest: national narrative revivals elsewhere are working precisely because they are exclusionary. A European story that is aspirational without being defined against someone is the harder construction, and we should not assume it is available just because it is preferable.
