# New European Narrative and Consciousness — brief

## Current state

_No primary sources filed yet._

The starting position from cross-referenced material: Europe's narrative deficit is not a shortage of
stories but a shortage of stated destinations. Political communication across the continent has settled
into a defensive register — securing pensions, securing peace, securing prosperity — which is a promise
to preserve, not an invitation to build. A continent that only offers preservation cannot produce
aspiration, and the field's guiding question (an aspirational story of our continent) therefore depends
on something upstream of storytelling: a collective answer to who we are together and what we want to
become. That question is currently being answered mostly by more radical actors, because elected
leadership has vacated it.

## Cross-referenced finds

- **Goal-based political leadership** (Nov 2025, own writing) — argues that goal-avoidance is not only conflict-avoidance but a symptom of a pluralistic, individualistic society with no shared answer to "who are we, together?". Relevant here for the identity layer the essay explicitly defers to a companion text, and for the confidence mechanism it cites from El-Mafaalani: societies with a positive outlook on the future can endure hardship; the spiral of distrust and radicalisation starts only when belief in a better tomorrow erodes. ## Open questions / what to watch next

- The announced companion essay on collective identity — local and European — is the piece this field most needs. It is unwritten.
- Can an aspirational European story be told without a shared goal to point at, or does narrative here necessarily follow direction rather than lead it?
- Who currently supplies Europe's most-heard story of itself, and on what emotional register? We should be able to name the competition precisely.
