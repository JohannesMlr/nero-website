## Cross-referenced finds

- **Goal-based political leadership** (Nov 2025, own writing) — the citizenship-relevant claim is that negotiating goals is not merely a steering mechanism but an identity-building and activating process that includes and informs citizens and "makes democracy tangible and alive". Where that process is absent, democracy becomes quiet and mechanical, and goal conflicts — which is what all social conflicts ultimately are — get amplified in echo chambers instead of resolved. Also relevant: the distinction between who benefits from a goal and who must contribute to it, which is a citizenship question as much as a communication one. Primary entry: `../03-new-state-and-governance/sources/2025-11-23-goals-as-political-leadership-instrument.md`

## Open questions / what to watch next

- What is the concrete European format for citizens participating in goal-setting rather than only in elections — and does any existing citizen-assembly practice actually produce goals that bind?
- If every social conflict is a goal conflict, what does productive public conflict over goals look like, as opposed to the echo-chamber version?
