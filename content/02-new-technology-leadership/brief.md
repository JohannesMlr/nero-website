# New Technology Leadership — brief

## Current state

The competitiveness diagnosis is now consensus at the top of European economic and political life. What is still missing is delivery. Two years after the 2024 Draghi report set out roughly 170 proposal clusters across energy, industry, defence, digital infrastructure, capital markets and productivity, implementation is close to flat: the European Policy Innovation Council's Draghi Observatory counted 60 of 383 tracked recommendations fully implemented by July 2026 — 15.7 per cent, up 0.6 percentage points in six months.

The headline metric of the gap is unchanged and keeps being cited as the shorthand for it: **four of the world's fifty largest technology companies are European**. The framing around that number has shifted, though, and in a direction that matters for us. The argument is no longer industrial prestige or "digital sovereignty" as a regulatory posture. It is fiscal: a Europe that does not grow cannot fund defence, healthcare, pensions, education and the climate transition, and a continent that cannot fund those things loses strategic independence regardless of what it legislates. Technology leadership is being argued as an input to sovereignty, not as a sector.

The newest institutional response is the **Rhine Group** (August 2026), co-chaired by Mario Draghi and Patrick Collison and directed by Luis Garicano — a deliberately mixed forum of economists, founders and former officeholders whose only stated job is to convert the Draghi agenda into reforms someone actually enacts. It has no formal standing and does not claim to substitute for the Commission or national governments. Notably, it argues from offence rather than protection: rebuild the capacity to invent and scale, rather than defend the installed base. Its composition is founder-heavy in a way previous European reform bodies were not.

A second front has opened almost immediately, and it is about standing rather than substance. The Rhine Group is registered under Swiss law with offices at a Geneva trust company, runs its own site on Webflow and AWS infrastructure in Northern Virginia, counts no Polish members and exactly one from any post-2004 accession state. For a body whose case rests on Europe's capacity to build and to govern itself, that is not a footnote — it is the first counter-argument anyone reaching for a rebuttal will use, and it lands before the policy content is discussed. The lesson generalises past this one organisation: in this field, credibility is now partly a question of whether an institution runs on what it says Europe should be able to build.

The open question is whether this class of intervention works at all. The Rhine Group exists precisely because reports did not move the machine; it is now betting that convening power plus commissioned evidence can. The flat implementation record is simultaneously the justification for the bet and the strongest evidence against it.

## Open questions / what to watch next

- Does the Rhine Group produce **named, costed, politically sponsored reform packages** picked up by a specific government or Commissioner — or another well-argued document? First real test likely at its first convening.
- Does the EPIC Draghi Observatory implementation index move measurably over the next two quarters, and does any movement trace back to advocacy of this kind?
- Where does the founder-heavy composition push the agenda relative to the original report — toward permitting, procurement and scale-up conditions, or toward capital-market and listing reform (cross-ref Field 04)?
- Is "only 4 of the top 50" the right metric at all, or does it flatter platform market cap over industrial and deep-tech capability where Europe still has positions worth compounding?
- Does the group fix its own legitimacy exposure — European domicile, European infrastructure, representation from the post-2004 member states — or does it let "Swiss-registered, US-hosted, no Poland" become the standing rebuttal?
- Watch for the counter-coalition: incumbent-protection arguments will not disappear because a manifesto rejects them.
